How to Play Tunk and Pitty Pat

Family Fun is a free browser card game by Albert Q. Lightfoot. You pick one of two family card games — Tunk or Pitty Pat — and play 1v1 against Dee or in a 4-player game against Marcus, Dee, and Trey. Every match is played for points, never for money. Points cannot be bought, sold, or cashed out. This page explains both games in full: the deal, the turns, the scoring, how a hand ends, and the decisions that separate a careful player from a lucky one.

Tunk: the deal

Tunk uses one standard 52-card deck with no jokers. Two or four players sit at the table and everybody is dealt 5 cards. The next card is turned face up to start the discard pile, and the rest of the deck becomes the stock. The goal of every hand is to get rid of your unmatched cards — the cards that are not part of a spread — because unmatched cards are what cost you points when the hand ends.

Tunk: your turn

At the very start of your turn, before you draw, you may drop: lay your hand face up and claim that you hold the lowest unmatched total at the table. Dropping is the boldest move in Tunk, and it is covered in its own section below. If you do not drop, you draw one card — either the top card of the stock or the top card of the discard pile. After drawing, you may lay down spreads from your hand and hit spreads other players have already laid down. Finally, you discard one card face up, and the turn passes to the next player.

Tunk: spreads and hitting

A spread is either three or four cards of the same rank (for example, three 7s) or three or more cards in a row in the same suit (for example, the 4, 5, and 6 of hearts). Aces are low only: A-2-3 is a run, but Q-K-A is not. Cards in a spread no longer count against you. You can also hit another player's spread — if someone has laid down three 7s and you hold the fourth 7, you may play it onto their spread on your turn. Hitting is often the fastest way to shed a heavy card, because a card played onto a spread is gone for good.

Tunk: Al's points

Family Fun scores cards with Al's points system, the same values used at his family table:

CardsPoints
Number cards (2 through 9)5 points each
10s, Jacks, Queens, Kings10 points each
Aces15 points each

A starting hand that totals exactly 50 points — for example, five 10-point cards — is a natural Tunk. Show it at once and you win double. Spreading every card in your hand with no card left to discard is a tonk out, which also wins double. Going out on a final discard, where your last unmatched card leaves your hand as the discard, wins single.

Tunk: dropping

Dropping is a claim, made at the start of your turn, that your unmatched total is the lowest at the table. If you are right, you win the hand. If another player ties you or holds a lower total, the drop fails and you pay double. That risk is the heart of Tunk: drop too early and you pay for it; wait too long and someone else goes out first. If the stock runs out with nobody out, the lowest unmatched total wins a single stake.

Tunk: points at the end of every hand

When a hand ends, each loser's unmatched cards are added up, and the winner takes that many points from each loser. A loser never pays more points than they have — nobody's stack goes below zero. Everybody starts a match with 5 points. The first player to 150 points wins a 1v1 match, and the first to 300 wins a 4-player match. If your stack hits 0, you get a free top-up back to 5 for the next hand, so nobody is ever stuck watching from the sidelines.

Tunk strategy

Watch the discard pile. Every card another player throws away tells you something about what they are not holding. Shed your 10-point cards and Aces early when you can, because a single Ace left unmatched at the end costs 15 points. Keep flexible cards — a card that can join two different spreads is worth more than a card that fits only one. And count before you drop: add up your unmatched total, remember which heavy cards are still unseen, and only drop when the table has shown you enough to believe you are lowest.

Pitty Pat: the deal and the goal

Pitty Pat is a pairing game. Each player is dealt 5 cards, and the goal is to hold three pairs. Pairs stay in your hand — unlike Tunk, you do not lay them down. Being dealt three pairs right away wins the hand on the spot. From there, every turn is about trading the cards that do not pair for cards that might.

Pitty Pat: your turn

On your turn you draw one card, from the stock or from the top of the discard pile, and then you discard one card. The moment your hand holds three pairs, you call Pitty Pat and the hand ends. The winner takes 15 points from each loser, and a loser never pays more points than they have. As in Tunk, the first player to 150 points wins a 1v1 match and the first to 300 wins a 4-player match.

Pitty Pat house toggles

Pitty Pat in Family Fun has house toggles on the table. "Include 8–10" is on by default and controls whether the 8s, 9s, and 10s are in the deck. Two bonus toggles are off by default: First Porter adds 10 points for a strong opening hand, and Last Low adds 10 points for finishing with the lowest unmatched card. Turn them on when your family plays that way; leave them off for the plain game.

Pitty Pat strategy

Pairs are the whole game, so protect them. Never break a pair to chase a prettier one unless the discard pile is offering you the exact card you need. Pay attention to what other players discard: a discarded 6 means nobody downstream is likely to be holding both other 6s, so a single 6 in your hand just got weaker. When you hold two pairs and one loose card, your hand is a coin flip away from winning — draw from the discard pile whenever the top card pairs with your loose card.

Levels, rewards, and Family Rooms

Points you win stack up toward levels, and each level unlocks a card style or a table theme for free — rewards are earned by playing, not bought. Family Rooms let you play at a private table with your own family: create a room, share the 6-letter code, and family members tap Family Rooms and type the code in to join your table. Family Rooms include moderated table chat and optional voice chat under the game's Keep It Cool rules.
